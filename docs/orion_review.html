Five-Lens Teardown · No Mercy Edition

Orion, under five
interrogation lamps.

A portfolio-intelligence dashboard for (mostly European) retail investors: ISIN-first, multi-asset, broker-CSV import, live prices stitched from Yahoo / OpenFIGI / ECB / CoinGecko, a Claude-powered "Intelligence" briefing, and a crisis "Stress Lab." Here's what a founder, a customer, an investor, a competitor, and an operator each think — bluntly.

Lens 01

The Founder

Vision · positioning · differentiation · is-this-a-company
SCORE  6 / 10

✓ Strengths

  • A genuinely sharp wedge. ISIN-first + ECB FX + DEGIRO/Saxo/Swissquote/UBS importers is a real European-retail-investor posture. Almost every polished competitor is US-ticker-centric. That's a defensible point of view, not a generic "track your stocks" clone.
  • Multi-asset net worth, not just equities. Folding bonds, property, crypto, metals, cash and debts into one canvas is the "single source of truth" pitch people actually want — most trackers stop at listed securities.
  • The narrative layer is the differentiator, not the data. A CIO-style morning briefing + "ask anything" is the one thing that feels like a product and not a spreadsheet. That's your story.

✗ Weaknesses

  • No positioning sentence. "Portfolio Intelligence Platform" is what everyone says. You can't tell me in one line who it's for and what it replaces. "The portfolio tool that finally understands European multi-broker investors" is a position; "intelligence layer" is a mood.
  • Feature sprawl is hiding the wedge. Command, Map, Analytics, Risk Profile, Stress Lab, Intelligence, Holdings, Markets — nine screens before product-market fit. That's a founder building for themselves, not for a market.
  • The moat is borrowed. Your "intelligence" is Claude, your prices are Yahoo, your FX is the ECB. Strip those and what's left that you own? Right now: the importers and the UX. Thin.
⚠ Biggest risk

You fall in love with the cockpit and never name the customer. A beautiful nine-screen tool with no one-line reason to exist is the single most common way ambitious solo products quietly die.

★ Most important improvement

Write the wedge down and cut to it: "The portfolio tracker built for European multi-broker investors, with an AI analyst on top." Then delete or hide every screen that isn't in service of that.

"You've built a product. You haven't yet decided who it's for — and that decision, not the tenth feature, is the whole job."

Lens 02

The Customer

Real need · frequency of use · willingness to pay
SCORE  6 / 10

✓ Strengths

  • It solves my actual mess. If I hold positions across DEGIRO + a Swiss bank + a crypto exchange, nobody shows me one consolidated, currency-correct number. Orion does. That pain is real and underserved.
  • "Enter once, never update prices" is the right promise. Manual spreadsheet upkeep is exactly why people abandon their tracker. Auto-pricing removes the chore that kills the habit.
  • The briefing gives me a reason to open it. A tracker is a destination I visit when anxious. A morning narrative about my holdings is a reason to come back daily — that's the difference between a utility and a habit.

✗ Weaknesses

  • I have to trust the numbers blindly. If one FX rate or one ISIN→ticker mapping is wrong, my net worth is wrong and I won't know. For money, "mostly right" feels worse than a spreadsheet I control. One bad number and I never trust it again.
  • My data lives in my browser. Clear cookies, switch laptops, or open it on my phone and it's gone. For something I'm supposed to use daily, that's disqualifying. I expected my portfolio to follow me.
  • Would I pay? Honestly, hesitant. I'll pay for something I trust with money and use weekly. A tool with no account, that might break when Yahoo changes, doesn't clear the trust bar for a credit card — yet.
⚠ Biggest risk

First wrong number = permanent distrust. With finance there's no "eh, close enough." A single visibly-wrong total and I churn and tell my friends it's broken.

★ Most important improvement

Make trust visible and make data portable. Show "price as of HH:MM, source X, FX from ECB," flag every fallback row loudly, let me override any number, and give me an account so my portfolio survives a browser.

"I don't need more dashboards. I need to believe the one number at the top is correct — and to find it on my phone tomorrow."

Lens 03

The Investor

Market size · scalability · monetization · defensibility · returns
SCORE  4 / 10

✓ Strengths

  • A real, underserved segment. European multi-broker DIY investors are a sticky, money-adjacent audience that incumbents (mostly US) ignore. Niches like this can support a profitable indie business.
  • Money-adjacent = high willingness to pay if trust is earned. People who track six-figure portfolios pay for tools that save time and reduce anxiety. The LTV ceiling is decent for a wedge product.
  • Lean cost base. Free data sources + a thin AI proxy means near-zero marginal cost per user today. Capital-efficient to validate.

✗ Weaknesses

  • No defensibility I can underwrite. No proprietary data, no network effect, no switching cost beyond data re-entry, no distribution edge. The AI layer is a wrapper anyone — including the brokers and Claude itself — can replicate in a weekend.
  • TAM is a side-project, not a fund-returner. "EU retail multi-broker net-worth tracker" is a real market but a small, fragmented, hard-to-reach one. At plausible price points and conversion, this caps out as a nice lifestyle business, not a venture outcome.
  • The foundation is a regulatory and reliability liability. Unofficial Yahoo scraping under a consumer financial tool is a ToS and continuity risk. As an investor, "your data layer can be turned off by a third party at any time" is an immediate red flag.
⚠ Biggest risk

No moat + no billing + a borrowed, brittle data layer. There's nothing here a funded competitor or an incumbent broker couldn't out-execute once the category is proven. I'm pricing in "easily copied."

★ Most important improvement

Show me retention and willingness-to-pay before you show me features. Get 50 paying users who open it weekly, then tell me a believable story for either (a) a defensible data/insight asset or (b) a profitable, capital-light niche I can back as such.

"This is fundable as a craftsman's business and unfundable as a venture bet. Decide which game you're playing before you raise — the answers are opposite."

Lens 04

The Competitor

How existing tools, brokers, and AI products beat you
THREAT  4 / 10 defensible

✓ What I can't easily take from you

  • The EU-native, ISIN-first posture. Most US incumbents would have to rebuild their core data model to match your importers and FX handling. That's real friction for them.
  • True all-asset consolidation in one clean UI. Stitching equities + bonds + property + crypto + metals + debts well is annoying; many of us punt on it. You've done the unglamorous integration work.
  • Taste. The interface and the briefing concept are nicer than a lot of utilitarian incumbents. Polish is a moat for exactly as long as it takes me to copy it.

✗ How I beat you

  • EU-native incumbents already own this lane. getquin, Parqet, and free open-source Portfolio Performance are ISIN-native, have communities, and (for the SaaS ones) accounts, sync, and paying users. Sharesight owns the dividend/tax-reporting angle globally. You're entering a furnished room.
  • The broker eats you for free. IBKR, DEGIRO and others show holdings, P&L and analytics inside the app the user already trusts with custody. "Free, already has my data, already has my money" beats "another tool I re-key into."
  • Your AI layer has no defense. I bolt a "portfolio briefing" onto my existing product with real custodial data, and it'll be more accurate than yours. Worse: the user can paste their holdings into ChatGPT or Claude and get 80% of your "Intelligence" for nothing. Your differentiator is the easiest thing to clone.
⚠ Biggest risk (to you)

You're sandwiched: incumbents have data + accounts + distribution above you, and a raw LLM gives "good enough" insight below you for free. The middle is a thin place to stand.

★ Most important improvement

Win one thing no incumbent does well and a raw LLM can't do: the cross-broker, currency-correct, all-asset consolidated view with insight grounded in verified numbers. Be the best at the consolidation a chatbot can't fake — not a prettier generic tracker.

"Everything you're proud of, I can copy. The only thing I can't easily copy is being the indispensable consolidation layer for someone with six accounts. Go live there or I win."

Lens 05

The Operator

Execution · data · cost · onboarding · support · daily ops
SCORE  4 / 10

✓ Strengths

  • The hard, boring engineering is real. ISIN checksum validation, country-aware ticker ranking, dual-listing dedupe, separate caches for prices vs. ETF sector weights, fallback chains that don't poison the cache — this is thoughtful, battle-scarred work, not a toy.
  • Failure-aware design. Tracking FX failures so rows can be flagged instead of silently showing local currency as dollars shows you've already met reality and respected it. That instinct is worth a lot.
  • Low run cost today. Stdlib server + free data + thin AI proxy is cheap to keep alive while validating. Sensible for a pre-PMF build.

✗ Weaknesses

  • The whole data layer is a house of cards. Unofficial Yahoo via yfinance, metals.live, free FMP tier — the code itself documents fighting shared-IP rate limits on Render. Any of these can change or block you with zero notice, and then everyone's numbers break at once. This is your #1 operational liability.
  • No real persistence or accounts. localStorage + in-memory caches means data dies on a cleared browser or a redeploy, there's no multi-device, no backup, and no path to billing. You can't operate a daily-use money tool on browser storage.
  • Support and correctness will eat you alive. Every odd ISIN, every illiquid secondary listing, every missing FX pair becomes a "your app says I lost €4,000" ticket. With finance, support load scales with edge cases, and edge cases scale with users.
⚠ Biggest risk

A silent upstream change (Yahoo/yfinance/metals.live) wrong-prices portfolios for everyone at once, and you find out from angry users, not monitoring. In a money tool, that's a reputation-ending event, not a bug.

★ Most important improvement

Harden the foundation before adding features: a licensed/redundant price source (or at least multi-source cross-checks + staleness alerts + monitoring), real accounts and a database, and a "data health" status surfaced to users. Stability is the feature for money.

"You engineered the clever 20% beautifully and skipped the unglamorous 80% — accounts, durable data, licensed prices, monitoring — that decides whether a finance product survives contact with real users."

Cross-Examination

Where the five agree, and where they fight.

⟡ Common patterns across all reviewers

  • The craft is real — engineering, taste, and the all-asset consolidation are genuinely strong. Nobody disputes the build quality.
  • The EU / ISIN-first wedge is the best asset — every lens independently circled it as the thing worth defending.
  • Trust and durability are the universal blocker — borrowed/brittle data, no accounts, browser-only storage. Customer, Investor, and Operator all stop at the same wall.
  • The AI "Intelligence" layer is the most loved and least defensible feature simultaneously.
  • Scope is too wide for the stage — nine screens before proof anyone uses one daily.

⚔ Biggest disagreements

  • Is the small market a bug or a feature? The Investor sees the niche TAM as a reason it's not venture-fundable; the Founder/Customer see the same niche as exactly why it can win. Both are right — for different games.
  • Is the AI layer a moat or a liability? The Founder treats it as the differentiator; the Competitor says it's the first thing to get copied (or replaced by a raw chatbot). Verdict: it's a hook, not a moat.
  • Build more vs. harden. The product instinct says ship the vision (Stress Lab, Risk Profile); the Operator/Customer say freeze features and fix trust, persistence, and data first.

✓ Top 5 changes to make next

  1. Write the one-line wedge and re-point the product at it: best portfolio tool for European multi-broker investors, AI analyst included.
  2. Earn trust visibly — source + timestamp on every price, loud flags on fallbacks/missing FX, user override on any cell.
  3. Add accounts + a real database. Portfolio must survive a cleared browser and follow the user to their phone.
  4. De-risk the data layer — multi-source cross-checks, staleness alerts, monitoring, and a plan toward a licensed feed for prices.
  5. Validate demand — get 30–50 people using it weekly and at least a handful paying, before building screen ten.
▶ Build first (the MVP)

One screen, done flawlessly: consolidated holdings + correct total net worth across brokers and currencies, with trustworthy, sourced, overridable numbers — plus accounts so it persists. Add the AI briefing as the single delight on top. That's the whole product worth testing.

✕ Remove / avoid building

Shelve Stress Lab, Risk Profile, Map, and the heavier "Analytics" until the core is trusted and used. They're impressive demos that add correctness surface area and support load while distracting from the one thing that earns retention. Don't ship more lenses onto numbers people don't yet trust.

⌖ What is this, really?

Startup: weak Side project / indie product: strong ✓ Consulting tool: weak Portfolio project: exceptional ✓✓

As a portfolio piece, this is already a strong yes — it demonstrates serious full-stack and product judgment. As a niche indie product / profitable side project for EU DIY investors, it's a credible path if you fix trust + persistence and find 50 weekly users. As a venture startup, not as built: no moat, small fragmented TAM, borrowed data.

Final Verdict
5.0 / 10
Founder 6 · Customer 6 · Investor 4 · Competitor 4 · Operator 4
⟲ SIMPLIFY  —  then re-position

Don't abandon it — the bones are too good. Don't keep building either. Cut to the core: one trustworthy consolidated net-worth view with accounts and an AI briefing, aimed squarely at European multi-broker investors. Pivot the positioning from "intelligence platform" to a sharp, owned wedge, and harden the foundation (trust, persistence, data resilience) before anything else. Prove weekly use and a little willingness-to-pay. Then — and only then — decide whether you're running a craftsman's indie business or chasing a startup. Today it's an outstanding portfolio project one disciplined cut away from being a real product.

"Your problem was never capability. It's focus. Build less, trust more, and name your customer."
