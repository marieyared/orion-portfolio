Executive Summary

This is a genuinely thoughtful portfolio — more sophisticated than most retail books I see — and it gets two structural things right that most people miss. But it has one tension at its core and one avoidable tax exposure, and it carries some lazy cash.

What's right: You hold US-equity exposure almost entirely through Irish-domiciled UCITS ETFs rather than US funds, and your bond sleeve is floating-rate / short-maturity. The first point is a deliberate, intelligent estate-tax structure (more on that below); the second means you carry almost no interest-rate duration risk at a time when rates are high and sticky. Someone here knows what they're doing.

The core tension: You told me the goal is long-term growth, ideally beating or matching the S&P 500, with a 20% maximum drawdown tolerance. But only ~58% of the book is in equities; the other ~42% sits in gold, short bonds, and cash. Those two goals fight each other. A portfolio that's 42% defensive cannot beat the S&P 500 unless your equity sleeve massively outperforms — which is the opposite of "match the index." You have to consciously choose which goal wins. I'll show you the trade-off in numbers.

The avoidable exposure: You hold ~$105,000 in individual US stocks plus a US-listed Bitcoin ETF. For a non-US person, US-situs assets above $60,000 are exposed to US estate tax of up to 40% on death. Your instinct to "keep individual US stocks near $60k" was exactly right — you're just $45k over the line today. The fix is clean and respects your wish to never sell Nvidia.

Bottom line: Don't tear this up. Three moves — convert the non-Nvidia US single stocks into the UCITS wrappers you already own, put the idle cash to work, and consciously set a target weight for gold — would tighten the book considerably without changing its character. Detail follows.

Reading the mix

For a 20+ year horizon with stable income and no liquidity needs, 58% equity is on the conservative side. Textbook glide-paths for that horizon and a 6–7/10 stated risk appetite would sit at 70–85% equity. Your defensive sleeve is doing two jobs at once: gold as a macro hedge, and short bonds/cash as dry powder.

That's a perfectly defensible capital-preservation-with-growth stance. It is not a "beat the S&P 500" stance. Be honest with yourself about which one you actually want — the allocation currently says the former while your words say the latter.

The maths of the tension

If your equity sleeve simply matches the S&P (say +8%/yr) and gold/bonds/cash return ~3%, the blended portfolio returns roughly +6%/yr — i.e. it structurally trails the index by the drag of the 42% defensive block.

To beat the index from here you need either more equity (which raises drawdown past your 20% comfort), or genuine stock-picking alpha (hard, and your own results are concentrated in two lucky-or-skilled names). There's no free lunch in that gap.

Re-examine the JPM Equity Premium Income ETF: it's an income/covered-call product fighting your "pure growth" objective — either accept it as a deliberate volatility-dampener or replace it with plain Nasdaq/growth exposure.

Final Recommendation

The verdict

This is a B+ portfolio with A-grade plumbing and one philosophical knot to untie. Don't rebuild it. Do three things, in order:

  • 1. Re-wrap the non-Nvidia US single stocks into your UCITS ETFs. Biggest risk-adjusted improvement; gets you back under the $60k estate line while keeping Nvidia and your full economic exposure. (Check the tax cost of selling first — Swiss private investors are typically capital-gains-exempt, but confirm for the actual owner.)
  • 2. Put the idle cash to work at ~4%. Free money you're currently leaving on the table.
  • 3. Pick one goal and set gold's target accordingly. "Hedge-and-grow" → keep ~20–25% gold and accept you'll track near the S&P. "Beat the S&P" → cut gold to 10–15% and lift equity to ~70%, accepting a deeper worst-case drawdown. You can't have maximum safety and index-beating returns from the same book.

Do those and you'd move from B+ to A–. The bones are good; the discipline is mostly already here. The work now is consistency between what the portfolio says and what you say you want.
